# Global-Maritime-Fleet
Maritime dashboard tracking global shipbuilding orders, voyage telemetry, fuel emissions, and port safety operations across commercial fleet routes.

## Project Description
This project is an interactive, multi page Power BI dashboard built to monitor global commercial shipping operations. Built on a clean Galaxy Schema data model, it connects shipyard orderbooks, live voyage telemetry, carbon emissions, and port safety audits. The report gives fleet owners and port authorities visibility into shipyard backlogs, fuel efficiency trends, choke point delays, and carrier inspection scores across international waters.

## Identified Business Challenges
Shipping companies have to meet IMO emission rules and deal with delays they can’t predict. It’s hard for fleet operators and charterers to connect shipyard spending with daily voyage performance and port compliance.

Late vessel deliveries can cost millions. Poor carbon ratings can lead to fines. Ships can get held at port after failed inspections.

This project puts everything in one dashboard. It tracks shipyard order backlogs, shows fuel emissions across trade routes, and flags port safety risks early, before they turn into financial losses.

## Key Insights & Findings
- **Shipbuilding is heavily concentrated in Asia:** South Korea ($22.2B), China ($20.2B), and Japan ($14.5B) control over half of the global $111.6B orderbook, with South Korea and China alone holding 38% of total contract value. 

- **Green technology adoption is still low:** Only 29.3% of the fleet currently uses green tech or autonomous features, meaning over 70% of vessels still run on traditional heavy fuels.  

- **Severe delivery backlog:** Only 17.8% of contracted vessels have actually been delivered so far, showing that most of the global orderbook remains under construction in shipyards.   

- **Southern reroutes cause the biggest delays:** The Cape of Good Hope recorded the highest transit delays at 1,355 hours, followed closely by the Strait of Magellan (1,283 hours) and the Strait of Hormuz (1,277 hours).

- **China dominates worldwide port traffic:** China recorded 191 port calls, nearly double the United States (105) and three times that of South Korea (63).

- **Carrier safety scores vary significantly:** Frontline Tankers earned the top safety score at 63, while Ocean Network Express was the only major carrier to average below 50.
